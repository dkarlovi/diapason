## Design specificity and impression

The interface is specific to a whistle-to-MIDI studio: live pitch, acoustic
calibration, retained original performance, and parallel audio/MIDI playback are
purposeful. The existing Slate & blue direction fits. The biggest opportunity
is continuity and recovery between making a take and getting usable MIDI.
There is no evidence here that a visual redesign is needed.

## Priority issues

1. **P1: No direct saved-take handoff.** `src/hooks/useCapture.ts:193` persists
   the take, then shows "Recording saved" for four seconds. The notice in
   `src/components/Capture.tsx:68` has no recording identity or Editor link.
   First-timers must infer Library -> find the new take -> Open. Keep the studio
   ready for another take, but provide a durable Open/Edit last take action.
   Evidence: source-confirmed interface gap; no completed capture observed.
   Suggested command: `/impeccable onboard`.
2. **P1: Save failure does not offer recovery of the captured performance.**
   `src/hooks/useCapture.ts:142` stops capture before saving; its catch at line
   198 asks the user to "record again to retry". There is no exposed retry-save
   or recover-audio action, and the error notice expires after six seconds.
   Retain the captured take and offer Retry save or Download original audio.
   Evidence: source-confirmed recovery gap; storage failure was not injected.
   Suggested command: `/impeccable harden`.
3. **P2: Unknown microphone settings are displayed as passed.** Under a
   synthetic stream omitting DSP settings, acknowledgment led to "Passed ·
   Unknown — browser does not report this setting" beneath labels claiming the
   processing is disabled. `src/hooks/useRecordingSetup.ts:103` treats accepted
   unknown settings as passed, and `src/components/StatusChecklist.tsx:24`
   renders that result. Distinguish verified from accepted-unverified, explain
   the consequence for whistle detection, and reserve troubleshooting controls
   for when needed. Browser observed with synthetic input; real-device
   prevalence unknown. Suggested command: `/impeccable clarify`.
4. **P2: MIDI export lacks a visible label.** In
   `src/components/EditorCanvas.tsx:100`, Download is an icon next to labeled Play.
   It has an accessible name/title, but touch and first-time users must infer
   which control delivers the promised outcome. `useEditorExports.ts:53` has an
   error state but no success state. Make Export MIDI visible; distinguish
   initiating a browser download from knowing the file was saved. Evidence:
   source review; no download observed. Suggested command: `/impeccable clarify`.

## Cognitive load and emotional journey

Setup is broadly grouped and progressive. The main concerns are technical
terminology, the connected microphone control cluster, and discovering the
save-to-Editor transition. The six-step progress indicator is not six competing
choices; do not remove necessary calibration solely to reduce a control count.
Of the reference's eight checklist items, minimal choices and progressive
disclosure are the two weak areas, chiefly in microphone/tempo setup. Treat this
as moderate load, subject to real first-use observation.

The entry is calm and actionable. Calibration can reassure but may become a
valley when users must interpret unknown browser processing. Capturing a melody
should be the peak; a temporary save notice does not connect that achievement
to refinement and export. The later emotional sequence is a prediction from
the interface, not participant research.
